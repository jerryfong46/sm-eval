What Is the Smith Maneuver?

The Smith Maneuver is a legal Canadian debt-conversion strategy that can turn non-deductible mortgage interest into potentially deductible investment-loan interest. It is usually built around a readvanceable mortgage (mortgage + HELOC).

How It Works

As mortgage principal is paid down, that same amount can be re-borrowed from the HELOC and invested in income-producing, non-registered investments. The HELOC interest may be deductible when CRA rules are met, and tax refunds can be recycled to accelerate the process.

How to Implement

  1. Set up a readvanceable mortgage structure.
  2. Track monthly principal paid on the mortgage.
  3. Re-borrow that amount from the HELOC and invest in qualifying assets.
  4. Maintain clean tracing and records for tax reporting.
  5. Use tax refunds based on your plan (mortgage, HELOC, reinvest, or cash).

Enhancing With Accelerators

Common accelerators include debt-swap style moves, dividend cash flow re-routing, and (for eligible business/rental cases) cash-flow-dam style structuring. These can increase deductible loan growth, but they also increase operational complexity and audit sensitivity.

Debunking Myths

You do not necessarily need portfolio return to exceed the HELOC stated rate to improve economics, because tax deductibility lowers after-tax borrowing cost. Also, the core maneuver is not the same as simply selling assets and re-buying them; that is a separate accelerator concept.

Risks and Considerations

Main risks are leverage, variable rates, market drawdowns, cashflow stress, behavioral mistakes, and tax-compliance errors. If home values fall or rates rise sharply, risk increases. Suitability depends on risk tolerance, discipline, and time horizon.

Tax-Free Savings Accounts

A TFSA is a tax-advantaged account, not a debt-conversion strategy. The Smith Maneuver relies on non-registered investing linked to borrowing-use tracing. They can both be useful, but they solve different planning goals.

FAQs

Is it risky? Yes, it uses leverage and variable rates, so risk management is essential.

Can it be used with rental/business contexts? In some cases, yes, with proper structuring and advice.

Do I need professional help? Strongly recommended for financing setup, tax tracing, and compliance.

The Bottom Line

The strategy can improve tax efficiency and long-term wealth for some households, but it is not one-size-fits-all. Good execution requires careful records, prudent risk control, and professional financial/tax guidance.
